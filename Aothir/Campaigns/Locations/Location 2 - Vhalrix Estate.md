# Roleplay Hooks

# About the Estate
## Outside
Nestled in the noble district at the heart of the capital, the Vhalrix Estate is a study in quiet authority. Unlike the sprawling palaces of more ostentatious houses, it exudes a subtler power, one that does not clamour for attention yet is impossible to overlook. The estate’s manicured grounds and elegantly weathered stonework speak of age, wealth, and patience. 

It is large, certainly; three floors in height, and stretching in three wings, but not vast for showmanship’s sake. Its architecture is purposeful, not indulgent. The structure curves slightly inward, forming a half-enclosed courtyard at its rear, with gravel paths winding through a modest but meticulously kept garden.

Though it may once have hosted banquets, councils, and courtships, the manor now breathes with hushed routine. No bells ring, no servants bustle. It is a house pared down to function and ritual, sustained more by memory than motion.

At the head of the family is [[Baron Edran Vhalrix]], a distinguished man in his early 60s. In his prime, the nobleman's taste for the courtly games and politics of the kingdom would diminish after the death of his wife. He has become quiet, but is still well respected for his restraint and intellect.
## Inside
Inside, the manor is cool and dim, even at midday. Tall windows are dressed in heavy drapes of aged velvet, most drawn just enough to let light through in ribbons rather than floods. The air carries the faint scent of parchment, old wood, and a trace of orange oil used to polish fixtures older than many of the city's laws.

Furniture is antique and beautifully made, but not recently replaced—worn in places by generations of use. Every hallway echoes slightly, not from emptiness, but from a design that once assumed more feet would tread its floors.

Subtle flourishes betray the house’s age: floorboards creak only in very specific spots, portraits gaze not directly at the viewer but slightly askance, and doors close with just a bit too much finality.

### Layout and Rooms
#### Ground Floor
- **Entrance Hall**: Marble-floored, with a high ceiling and restrained heraldry. A single iron chandelier holds dim magical light. Two Vhalrix ancestors look down from opposing portraits—both with the sigils in their rings painted over.
- **Drawing Room**: Used only when guests visit, this room is pristine but cold. One of the few places where fire is ever lit.
- **Dining Hall**: Seats ten at a long oak table, though rarely more than three dine at once.
- **Library**: Locked at all times. Lined with old tomes and estate ledgers, with a few inconspicuous gaps in the bookshelves. A narrow reading balcony leads to the first floor.
- **Servants’ Access Corridors**: Discreet routes between wings, half-hidden behind panel doors.
#### First Floor
- **Master Bedroom Suite**: Spacious, understated. The bed is high-backed and draped, but worn with age. A writing desk remains active, kept in order by the master servant.
- **Guest Rooms (3)**: Tastefully appointed but clearly unused. Linens folded, fireplaces cold.
- **Private Study**: Decorated with ancestral tokens. The only room with the **original crest of Threxis**, faintly visible beneath a newer family sigil.
- **Nursery Room** (long unused): Preserved, covered in dust cloths. A music box still works, but plays slightly off-tune.
#### Basement Floor
- **Wine Cellar**: Stone-walled and shadowed. Racks of vintages line the walls. A section of the back wall is slightly uneven—mortar newer, stone subtly misaligned. Behind it lies the **sealed passage to the vault**.
- **Pantry Storage**: Dry goods and preserved foods—well-stocked, but organized with military precision.
- **Servant Quarters**: Sparse, austere. Functional, not unkind.
## Household Staff
- **Master Servant**: _Gareth Thorne_, an ageing man with sharp memory and sharper intuition. Loyal, discrete, and painfully aware of the estate’s slow quieting.
- **Two House Servants**: Maintain the rooms, clean, carry messages. They rarely speak unless spoken to.
- **One Cook and Assistant**: Handle all meals. Meals are functional, not elaborate.
## Subtle Details
- A hallway mirror is older than the estate itself, said to reflect “only the obedient.”
- In the guest wing, a doorframe bears scorched carvings beneath the paint—sigils that do not match any known noble crest.
- A clock in the drawing room chimes at odd hours, often marking moments of silence or tension.
- Some doors bear hinges of blackened bronze—a metal once reserved for temples.
- The family crest is always engraved, never gilded. The motto is long since chiseled out.
# Vhalrix Family
Seat of a minor but ancient noble house. The current family is wealthy but politically quiet, known for a lineage of “civic duty” and loyal service to the crown.

Though the Vhalrix name is rarely invoked in the city’s politics, those who know better understand: their silence is not weakness, but choice. The family holds considerable influence, should they ever care to wield it, and it is that restraint, more than any title, that commands respect.

**Secret Past**: The Vhalrix bloodline once served as custodians of sacred rites, bound to Threxis through ancestral pacts. After his fall, the family was pressured, politically and spiritually, to abandon their faith. In doing so, they sealed away what remained beneath their home.

# The Sealed Vault
## The Letter
In the desk draw of Edran's study is a letter. It is crisp and clean, but it's surface tells of its age. Not held within an envelope, it is miraculous that the paper is so well preserved. On its outside, in excellent penmanship, are the words "To whom may seek what has been sealed". Flipping the letter over, there is a wax seal pressed with some kind of sigil.

If the party have already been to [[Location 1 - Temple of Threxis]], then they may recognise the symbol if they have picked up on any of the iconography.

The sigil is 

> [!quote]- Letter Content
> To the one who finds this,
>
Know that you stand where I have never dared.
>
Beneath this house lies more than stone and secrecy. There is weight buried there — not just of history, but of _intention_. My ancestors placed something in the dark and prayed no one would ever seek to bring it into the light again. I have honored that silence. I have kept the door closed. But doors are not always meant to stay shut.
>
I do not know what you seek. Power, perhaps. Truth, if you’re lucky. Maybe simply something to sell. But before you take what lies below, I ask you to understand this:
>
My family once believed in a god who taught that suffering had purpose. That to choose pain in the name of others was the highest calling. I do not know if that was true. But I know this — those teachings gave my ancestors clarity, strength, and dignity… until they gave them justifications.
>
There is a line between sacrifice and cruelty. Those who sealed that vault saw it too late. If you do not see it at all, you will not understand what you awaken.
>
But if you _do_ see it… if you feel even a shadow of that weight pressing into your chest as you descend… then perhaps this letter has served its purpose.
>
Take the artefact. Take the truth with it. But carry it _knowing what it cost_ to bury.
