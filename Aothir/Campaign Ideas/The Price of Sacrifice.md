# Introduction
# Act I - Awakening
**Theme**: Discovery, Mystery, and Deception
The party is contacted by a mysterious cult led by **Jezia Luth**, who offers wealth and vague references to forgotten power.

- The cult has located two divine artefacts and needs skilled hands to retrieve them.    
- **Location 1**: The **Ruined Temple** of the fallen god — overgrown or sunken in a wasteland. Here, the party first encounters visual or spiritual remnants of the god.
- **Location 2**: A **sealed vault** beneath a former monastery, noble estate, or forgotten sanctuary. Possibly guarded by relics of the old faith or temporal defences.
- After acquiring these artefacts, the players are approached by **Vessa Thornbite**, a charismatic “artefact liberator” working for a private Collector. She demands a race for the next item.

**Tension**: Players don’t yet know the god’s true nature or Jezia’s endgame. They are tools in someone else’s plan.
# Act II - The Race Begins
**Theme**: Rivalry, Revelation, and Escalation

Vessa challenges the party to beat her to the next artefact. If they succeed, she’ll share her knowledge of another location. If not, she demands one of the artefacts as payment.

- The party travels across dangerous terrain where **travel checks** determine how quickly they arrive. If they fail, roll 1d4 to see how long it takes Vessa to catch up.    
- **Location 3**: A ruined or shadowed shrine, wrapped in illusions or spiritual obscurity.
- **Location 4**: An ancient royal tomb or site of judgement — possibly beneath a still-ruling monarchy.
- The party begins to experience the weight of the artefacts: visions, whispers, and a creeping sense of being watched.

**Tension**: The cult provides increasingly direct “instructions.” Vessa’s motives blur — she may not be the villain Jezia painted her as.

# Act III - Fractures and Factions
**Theme**: Choice, Consequence, and Conflict
Word spreads. Rival cult cells, mercenaries, and religious orders become aware of the artefact hunt.
    
- The party must contend with **moral dilemmas**, **escalating divine interference**, and factions seeking to destroy or claim the artefacts.
- **Location 5**: A sacred pilgrimage site or martyr’s field, long lost to time. The party is tested emotionally or spiritually.
- **Location 6**: A memory-drenched sanctum or mirrored ruin where characters face visions of alternate selves or futures.
- Characters who have been changed by the artefacts begin to manifest traits of the god — powers, flaws, or compulsions.

**Tension**: The party starts questioning Jezia’s intent. The line between saviour and tyrant blurs. Vessa may propose destroying the artefacts or using them for a different purpose.
# Act IV - Unmaking or Ascension
**Theme**: Legacy, Temptation, and Final Choice

The location of the **seventh artefact** is uncovered, possibly by merging knowledge from all parties.
- **Location 7**: A divine forge, heart of a volcano, or celestial fault-line — the final heartbeat of the god.
- Jezia makes his move: either completing the rite to **absorb** the god’s essence or become a vessel for its rebirth.
- The artefacts resonate. Some party members may be tempted to use them. Others may want to destroy them.
- **Final Decision**: The party must choose:
    - Fulfil the ritual (and decide who becomes the vessel),
    - Destroy the artefacts (and deal with divine backlash),
    - Scatter the artefacts again (facing pursuit forever),
    - Side with Vessa, the Collector, or a third unexpected force.

**Endgame Variables**: Divine avatars, reborn gods, fractured pantheons, or a new age where mortals claim the divine mantle.

# Rhetoric - The Severance
Speech from Jezia if asked about the event that led to the creation of the artefacts or god's sundering:

“Ages have passed, empires have fallen, and the names of those responsible for the Severance have long passed into folklore and mythology. As histories are often written by the victors, sometimes not writing the history is the greatest slight of all. The victors in this case have allowed a divine being—one who once held the hearts of nations—to fall into obscurity. A heinous crime.”

_He pauses—measured, not dramatic._

“Threxis was not a tyrant. He was clarity given form. He did not command cruelty—he demanded purpose. The world asked him how to endure what must be endured. And he answered. Through sacrifice. Through cost. Through truth.”

“The Severance was not born of righteousness—it was born of fear. The gods saw a power among mortals that did not beg for mercy, did not plead for miracles. It _chose_. It acted. It took what was needed and gave what was owed. That power threatened their order, and so they scattered it, splintered it, sealed it away in the name of virtue.”

“They called it a mercy. But tell me this—what god is cast down in silence if not one they know would still draw followers if the truth were known?”

“The artefacts are not weapons. They are **remnants**. Proof. Each a breath of divinity held in quiet defiance of the lie. And now, we find ourselves in an age where the world is again uncertain. Where sacrifices are being made with no guidance, no balance. The old world cast aside the one voice that knew how to weigh such things.”

_He steps closer, voice lowering as if sharing a confidence._

“You have touched the artefacts. You’ve felt what lingers within them. That is not domination. That is not madness. That is **purpose**—and it is waiting.”

## Modern Reinterpretation (Jezia’s View)
To Jezia and his cult, Threxis was not corrupted — he was punished for clarity.
- They view the god’s silence as proof of restraint, his still-beating will as proof of necessity.
- They believe that _in a world of chaos, compromise, and cowardice_, only Threxis understood what it truly means to lead.
Jezia teaches:
- “Threxis never asked for obedience — only understanding.”
- “The Severance was not justice. It was fear, gilded in righteousness.”
- “We do not bring him back to rule. We bring him back to weigh the world once more.”

## Responses
### Rejection to the quest - No artefacts
_Jezia’s expression stills. Not anger—just stillness, like a man folding away a hopeful letter left unread._

“I see.”

_A pause, not for effect, but as if weighing a quiet sadness._

“I don’t fault you. The stories they buried him under were masterfully written. To turn away from what’s unfamiliar - what’s uncomfortable - that is human. Predictable, even.”

_He turns slightly, gesturing around the chamber, or to the unseen world beyond it._

“But whether you walk with me or not… the world continues to bleed. Its kings still burn lives like kindling. Its priests still pray for peace, then bless the sword. And all the while, the one god who ever demanded we take responsibility for that cost remains in pieces.”

_He steps toward you - not menacing, simply resolute._

“So walk away. For now. Just know this: others will come. Others always do. Some seeking truth. Some power. Some purpose.”

_And then, with complete certainty:_  
“And when you find yourself standing in the ashes of what should have been prevented… you will remember this moment, and you will remember that I asked _first_.”

_He turns, robes whispering like breathless parchment._

“I bear no grudge. Clarity takes time. You’ll return when you’re ready.”

_His gaze sharpens—not wild, but focused like a blade just before the strike._

“And what then? Will you hide them? Bury them again, so the world can keep bleeding without knowing why? Do you think that ends this?”

“You hold what I cannot afford to lose. And I—”

_He lifts his hand slightly. Not in violence yet, but the air in the room shifts. The moment tightens._

“You will hand them over. Or you will force me to become the villain you fear I already am.”

 “Do not mistake my patience for passivity. I invited you to become part of something greater. I gave you truth. Purpose. And you would cast it aside because it frightened you.”

 _He looks to the artefacts in your possession—not with greed, but with reverence. With longing. And a rising resolve._

### Rejection to the arguments
_He exhales slowly, a patient smile forming as though he's heard this before._

“Ah. The comfort of the story we were taught.”

“That suffering is evil. That sacrifice is savagery. That power must always be feared when it is not sanctioned by robes and crowns and heavenly thrones.”

_He steps forward just slightly—inviting, not threatening._

“Let me ask you - is it evil when a mother barters her life to save her child? When a soldier bleeds in place of his commander? When a leader chooses to let a few die so that thousands could live?”

“Of course not. We call that nobility. We call that courage. But Threxis? He codified it. He gave it weight. Gave it shape. And when he dared to ask the same from gods as he did from mortals, they called him evil.”

_His gaze narrows, not in aggression, but in clarity._

“If you fear him, then you understand him. If you hate him, then you admit he asked something you weren't ready to give. But do not mistake your discomfort for proof of wickedness.”

_He folds his hands calmly._

“We were given a world of war, plague, famine—and told to find meaning in it. Threxis offered that meaning. The others offered silence.”

_And then, with unsettling warmth:_  
“So tell me again—who is truly cruel?”

# History - The Fall of Threxis

Threxis, God of Sacrificial Purpose, was once the deity to whom kings, generals, and philosophers turned when their decisions came at great cost. Offerings were made to Threxis to ensure that the sacrifices - both personal and collective - would achieve their intended outcomes. Where other gods governed ideals, Threxis concerned himself with results.

His teachings echoed the cold voice of conscience that whispers, “You cannot save them all,” and “The ends justify the means.” Not a cruel god, but one of unflinching clarity, Threxis offered guidance stripped of sentiment. His clergy often consoled rulers with truths such as, “All power has cost,” and “To preserve one life is noble. To sacrifice one to save many is divine.” To common folk, facing hardship and grief, they would speak: “Purpose is measured in the pain we endure,” or “The highest devotion is knowing the blade will fall - and placing your neck beneath it anyway.”

As a result, temples to Threxis were not grand monuments in city hearts, but somber sanctuaries, often near gates, crossroads, or battlefields - places where decisions bore weight. Shrouded in dim light and the curling breath of fragrant incense, his temples were hushed places of reflection, where even a whispered breath could seem too loud.

In time, the ideology of sacrifice took on a literal form. Those on the brink of death would willingly offer their remaining life essence to empower the living. At first, it was a sacred act - dying warriors passing strength to successors, devout nobles gifting vitality to their heirs. The rite required explicit consent, and to perform it without was considered blasphemy. Those who gave themselves would whisper their assent through failing breath, and their bodies would be marked with ritual wounds, divine sigils, and implements of binding - spikes, cords, and needles.

But as generations passed and wars raged on, the doctrine twisted. Consent became inconvenient. On the battlefield, priests began uttering warped logic: “Is it not better that one unwilling soul fall, than ten willing ones die?” What began as a holy rite of transference became a utilitarian tool, and Threxis - unchanging, unyielding - watched, and did not intervene.

When his high priests began enacting blood rites indiscriminately, the other gods took notice. They empowered mortals, selecting seven champions - disillusioned former followers of Threxis - to turn against their god. In secret, the gods whispered fragments of a ritual to each of them, a rite known only in full as The Severance. Each champion received a part aligned with a divine principle, and together they fractured Threxis’ power, sealing it within seven sacred artefacts.

What scraps of that day remain suggest Threxis offered no plea, no denial. His final words, if true, remain etched in uneasy legend:  
“You do not fear what I have become. You fear that I am right.”

The artefacts were taken to sanctuaries and temples of the gods who aided in his downfall. As centuries passed, and wars and theft scattered the pieces, the name Threxis slipped into obscurity. The sacred artefacts, once symbols of sacrifice and sin, became lost to time.

Yet in forgotten corners and hidden halls, his name is still spoken - chanted in secret rituals, written in blood. In these places, unwilling victims feed rites of power, and his faithful whisper his epithets like prayers:
- He Who Weighs the Worth of Pain
- The Wound That Speaks
- Lord of the Measureless Price
- Chainbearer of the Greater Good

Those who study ancient religion or occult folklore refer to the event only as The Severance, or The Fall of Threxis. Little evidence remains, as his temples were systematically razed in the years following his defeat. Still, a few scattered fragments of his tenets have survived - enough to keep his memory alive in shadow.

## Clerical Doctrine – The Four Tenets of Threxis (Pre-Fall)
1. Balance the Blade – Every gain must have a cost. Every decision must be weighed.
2. Stand in the Place of Pain – The faithful do not look away from suffering. They choose when it must be borne.
3. No Gift Without Blood – Nothing sacred is free. Purpose demands price.
4. Die For Another, Live For All – The highest act of faith is sacrifice made with purpose.

# The Prophecy of the Threxis
A recovered fragment, chanted by Jezia’s inner circle, said to be pulled from the original Severance texts, misinterpreted or deliberately re-read through reverence

> **Seven were the lights unlit,  
> Bound in blade, in bone, in breath.  
> Shorn from crown, the blood went still—  
> But still it dreams beneath the depths.
> 
> When red runs not for death but need,  
> And hollow hands draw forth the flame,  
> The wheel shall turn, the wound shall speak,  
> And call the lost to speak its name.
> 
> From broken paths the chosen rise,  
> Not saint, nor kin, nor tethered thrall—  
> But hands that bear the severed sky,  
> And voices raised where none recall.
> 
> Then shall the lights be lit anew,  
> By those who walk with steps unseen.  
> The throne unmade shall shape its hue,  
> In silence, deep, and hands made clean.**

## Mantra of the Bound Flame
> **Seven lights, once torn, shall burn.  
> Hands unclean must not return.  
> Speak the name the world forgot—  
> Blood be bound, and purpose wrought.**

### Variants
**Quiet Prayer (whispered in solitude):**
> _"Flame in silence, heart in chain—  
> Let the wound be whole again."_

**Ritual Chorus (repeated in group ceremonies):**
> _"Light the dark, bind the flame.  
> Speak the truth, bear the name."_

## Prophecy Notes
- "Seven lights unlit": The artefacts, each containing a divine fragment.
- "Bound in blade, in bone, in breath": Hints at the physical diversity of the artefacts and the nature of Hemalurgy.
- "Red runs not for death but need": Suggests a re-framing of bloodletting as sacred duty rather than violence.
- "The wheel shall turn": Could refer to cycles of godhood, fate, or the return of divine order.
- "Hands that bear the severed sky": The party, unknowingly chosen, carry pieces of a god once torn from the heavens.
- "Throne unmade shall shape anew": Implies the god will return changed — possibly benevolent, possibly worse.

## Gauntlet of the Bound Fist
**Aspect**: Power  
**Form**: A bronze gauntlet with clawed fingers, inscribed with divine symbols. Warm to the touch and unnaturally heavy.  
**Effect**:
- Once per long rest, deal maximum damage on a melee weapon attack and knock the target prone (Strength save to resist).
- While attuned, your Strength score increases by 2, to a maximum of 22.
- Your unarmed and weapon attacks count as magical for overcoming resistance.  

**Curse – Wrath Unbound**:  
When you are below half your maximum HP, you must succeed a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw or immediately use your action to attack the nearest creature, friend or foe.  

**Recommended Location**: Act I – Ruined Temple of the fallen god.  
**Class Synergy**: Fighters, Barbarians, Paladins.

---
## The Breath Between Worlds
**Aspect**: Wisdom  
**Form**: A ceremonial key fashioned from bone, gold thread, and obsidian, with an ancient eye sigil at its base.  
**Effect**:
- Once per long rest, you may ask a question and receive a cryptic prophetic vision. This functions like a Commune spell but is flavored as dreamlike symbolism.
- Gain +2 bonus to Arcana and Insight checks.
- Once per day, gain advantage on one saving throw against an illusion or enchantment spell.  

**Curse – Fractured Sight**:  
After completing a long rest, roll a d6. On a result of 1, you suffer disadvantage on your first Wisdom-based check or save that day as echoes of alternate timelines confuse your perception.  
**Recommended Location**: Act I – Vault beneath a monastery or noble estate.  
**Class Synergy**: Wizards, Druids, Clerics.

---
## Lantern of Wakeful Light
**Aspect**: Guidance  
**Form**: A rusted lantern whose pale blue flame never flickers, even in water or wind.  
**Effect**:
- Reveals invisible or ethereal creatures within 30 feet while lit.
- Once per long rest, you may cast either Beacon of Hope or Daylight (no components required).
- Allies within 10 feet of you gain a +1 bonus on saving throws against being charmed or frightened.  

**Curse – Burden of the Watcher**:  
You cannot benefit from magical sleep or long rest effects unless you are the last to fall asleep and the first to awaken. You feel a compulsive need to stand watch, even in safe environments.  
**Recommended Location**: Act II – Abandoned shrine shrouded in magical darkness.  
**Class Synergy**: Rogues, Rangers, support Clerics.

---
## The Hollow Crown
**Aspect**: Judgement  
**Form**: A black iron circlet with thorn-like protrusions, light yet oppressive to wear.  
**Effect**:
- Once per long rest, mark a target you can see as Judged. For 1 minute, they have disadvantage on saving throws against your spells and abilities.
- You gain advantage on Persuasion and Intimidation checks against Judged targets.
- While worn, gain +1 bonus to Charisma checks against creatures of lower level or CR.  

**Curse – Entitled Will**:  
Whenever a creature resists a command or reasonable suggestion from you, make a DC 15 Charisma saving throw. On a failure, gain one level of Arrogance (a stacking condition to a maximum of 3), imposing -1 per level to Insight and Deception checks. These penalties reset after a long rest.
**Recommended Location**: Act II – Catacombs beneath a ruined or active throne room.  
**Class Synergy**: Paladins, Warlocks, Sorcerers.

---
## Veil of the Weeping God
**Aspect**: Compassion  
**Form**: A translucent veil with faint traces of dried tears that shimmer in starlight.  
**Effect**:
- Once per long rest, you may transfer up to 30 points of damage from a creature within 30 feet to yourself as a reaction.
- Gain +2 bonus to Persuasion and Medicine checks.
- Any healing spell you cast restores an additional 1d4 hit points.  

**Curse – Empath’s Burden**:  
Whenever you heal another creature using magic, you take psychic damage equal to half the amount healed. This damage ignores resistance and cannot reduce you below 1 HP.  
**Recommended Location**: Act III – Pilgrimage site desecrated by time or war.  
**Class Synergy**: Bards, Sorcerers, Clerics.

---
## Mirror of Reverence
**Aspect**: Memory  
**Form**: A hand-held silver mirror with a long crack through its surface. Reflections occasionally differ from reality.  
**Effect**:
- Once per day, you may replay a memory you personally witnessed and reroll an Intelligence-based check related to it. Alternatively, glimpse a historical moment tied to a place or object.
- Gain +2 bonus to History and Religion checks.  

**Curse – Echoes of What Could Be**:  
Once per day when you see your reflection, roll a d20. On a 1 or 2, you see yourself committing a terrible act in a future that may yet come. You suffer disadvantage on your next Charisma-based check as your confidence wavers.  
**Recommended Location**: Act III – Sanctum of memory or mirrored ruin.  
**Class Synergy**: Bards, Wizards, Rogues.

---
## The Anvil-Heart
**Aspect**: Will  
**Form**: A blackened iron ingot that pulses with a slow heartbeat. Worn over the chest or strapped to the back like a brooch.  
**Effect**:
- When reduced to 0 hit points, you may remain conscious and act normally for 1 additional round (once per long rest).
- You gain advantage on saving throws against gaining exhaustion.
- You ignore the penalties from your first level of exhaustion.
- You have advantage on Constitution saving throws made to maintain concentration.  

**Curse – Weight of Resolve**:  
After every combat encounter in which you drop below half your maximum HP, make a DC 15 Constitution saving throw. On a failure, gain 1 level of exhaustion unless you immediately spend at least 2 hit dice or sacrifice something narratively meaningful.  
**Recommended Location**: Act IV – Divine forge or final battlefield.  
**Class Synergy**: Monks, Fighters, tanks of any kind.
