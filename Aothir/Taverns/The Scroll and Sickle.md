# About
**Type**: Public Debate Forum  
**Theme**: Logic, Rhetoric, Philosophy, and Policy  
**Recognition**: **Iron Quill** – a stylised writing instrument worn as a lapel pin

**Description**:  
Where the academic district meets the civic, a spot that sticks out can be seen. Marbled stone benches and curved steps cut an imposing sight of political and historic spectacle. Students sit facing their teachers deep in debate, political adversaries do battle over policy, and budding philosophers quarrel over the minutia of life.

Anyone new to the city may mistake this simply as a sunken amphitheatre for the mundane thespian bands that pass through for a summer festival or winter solstice pageant. Those who sit on its steps and benches know its importance. Stopping at a simple open air cabana style bar with a sign reading The Scroll and Sickle, this amphitheatre, with its walled courtyard and tree-shaped plaza, is a location almost as old as the city itself. The Sickle has become a landmark location for government and academia alike. Important issues of local and foreign policy have been decided upon what is now called The Stone of Reason, an immense rectangular marble slab that shows the wear from generations of politicians, professors and philosophers who have walked upon it. Their prolific quotes are carved in painted lettering on its side, showing the impact this location has had on the people of this city. 

Wrought-iron lampposts line the walkways providing light while magical scribe orbs and enchanted quill and ink float through the space capturing the most profound of speeches. Seasonal awnings or trellises covered in Wisteria provide shade or warmth depending on the time of year.

Drunk nobles quote old poets, apprentices listen to the teachings of their masters, and those who deal in spy tradecraft pass secretive messages to their counterparts in its shadows. Though men do not throw punches here, there is no mistaking that this is a place of battle for those who wield their intellect as a weapon. 

Behind the bar stands a wisened old gentleman, [[Sammael Astos]]. Though not overly impressive or distinct, those who frequent the establishment regularly know the man to be the descendant of the original owner. The responsibility of caring for the location has passed from farther to son for as long as the city has stood. In his duty and under his care, he has heard and debated more than any who walk the government halls of the civic district. His sharp tongue is a match for any who sit at his bar and his opinion is respected by many who walk its paths.

**How It Works**:  
Any may take the **Stone of Reason** to speak. To earn the Iron Quill, one must defeat a current holder in debate—judged by an assembled panel of peer Quills. No spells, no tricks—just words. Holders of a Quill are viewed as wise thinkers, often consulted by councils, academics, and lawmakers. A single speech can elevate a farmer to the senate.
