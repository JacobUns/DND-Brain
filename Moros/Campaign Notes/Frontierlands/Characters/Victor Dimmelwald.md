# Backstory
A human in his twenties, Victor is the adopted son and apprentice of archaeologist [[Earlian Dimmelwald|Earlian]]. The two travelled north in search of artefacts of the ancient ruins revealed after the cooling of the northern hemisphere. The two live in [[Pefergee]], where Victor works at [[Pefergee#The Last Bed|the Last Bed]] with [[Karrik Sylvari, last of the Aurora'lyn|Karrik]].

# Xinderoth Description
Victor’s skin has lost all warmth of humanity. It is grey — not the healthy dusk of a drow, but the ash-pale grey of stone leeched of colour. It catches the light strangely, dull and matte, as though it refuses to reflect the world around it. Beneath the skin faint veins glow intermittently with pale luminescence, like cracks in cooling obsidian.

His eyes are piercing, silver-grey washed with white, without visible pupils. They burn with an intensity that makes it impossible to tell where he is looking; the gaze is steady, predatory, unblinking.

Hair, once dark, is now silver-white, falling straight and fine like spun glass. It moves with an uncanny stillness — when the air stirs, the strands resist, as if bound by their own subtle weight.

His features remain recognisable but are honed to unnatural symmetry. Cheekbones too high, jawline too exact, lips carved thin. There is no softness: he looks sculpted, as if entropy itself has stripped away imperfection.

His frame is lean and wiry, every muscle defined with ruthless economy. The proportions are slightly wrong — arms a touch too long, hands tapered to narrow, precise fingers. Fingernails shimmer faintly metallic, catching light like tiny shards of steel.

When he moves, the effect is unnerving. No wasted motion, no human hesitancy — every step lands with inevitability. Shadows seem to cling to him, not in a magical sense but as though the world itself dims around his outline.

When Netherbane is at his side, it resonates with him: the veins beneath his skin throb faintly in time with the blade’s dark radiance, completing the impression that Victor in this form is less a man than the embodiment of a creed forged long ago — survival through entropy, perfection through the erasure of weakness.
