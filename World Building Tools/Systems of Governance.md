The types of rulership or governance can be categorised broadly based on how authority and power are distributed and exercised. Here are some of the main types:
# Monarchy
- A single person (monarch) rules for life, often inheriting their position.
- Variants:
    - **Absolute Monarchy**: The monarch has unchecked power (e.g., historical France under Louis XIV).
    - **Constitutional Monarchy**: The monarch's powers are limited by a constitution or parliament (e.g., the United Kingdom).
# Democracy
- Power rests with the people, typically exercised through voting.
- Variants:
    - **Direct Democracy**: Citizens directly participate in decision-making (e.g., ancient Athens).
    - **Representative Democracy**: Citizens elect officials to make decisions on their behalf (e.g., the United States).
# Oligarchy
- Rule by a small group of people, often based on wealth, family ties, or military control.
- Example: Some historical city-states like Sparta in ancient Greece.
# Authoritarianism
- Centralised power with minimal political freedom for citizens.    
- Often led by a dictator or a ruling party.
- Example: Historical regimes like that of Francisco Franco in Spain.
# Theocracy
- Governance based on religious principles or by religious leaders.    
- Example: Iran, where clerical leaders play a significant role in governance.
# Anarchy
- The absence of a formal governing authority.
- Often represents a transitional state rather than a stable system.
# Federalism
- Power is shared between a central government and regional or state governments.    
- Example: The United States and Germany.
# Socialism/Communism
- Emphasis on social ownership and equality, with the state managing resources and production.
- Variants: Some governments are more socialist (e.g., Scandinavian countries), while others align with communism (e.g., historical USSR).
# Tribal or Clan Leadership
- Decisions are made by elders or leaders of a community, often in smaller or indigenous societies.
# Totalitarianism
- A form of authoritarianism where the state seeks total control over all aspects of life, including culture, economy, and private life.
- Example: North Korea.
