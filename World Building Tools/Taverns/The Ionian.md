# About
**Type**: Bardic Lounge & Storyteller's Guild  
**Theme**: Song, Storytelling, Poetry, and Lore  
**Recognition**: **Resonant Pipes** – a magical token storing song, applause and audience esteem. A small set of silver panpipes that can be either worn as a pin, pendant or other adornment.

**Description**:  
Nestled in a grove-like amphitheater within the city, **The Ionian** is crafted from living trees, enchanted wood, and glowing stones that hum with residual harmony. Its vaulted canopy of woven branches channels acoustics perfectly, and the central stage floats inches above the ground—untethered and gently spinning when a story is especially moving. Audience benches encircle the stage like ripples in a pond, and lights drift through the air, pulsing in rhythm with each performance.

No posters or loud announcements line the walls; every seat is filled by word of mouth alone. This is a place of reverence for the spoken and sung word—whether it’s a battle hymn, a tragic epic, or a joke so perfect it silences the room.

On one side, a bar filled with exotic liquor flows as all manner of patrons from different classes in the city chat and mingle. The multi-tiered seating allows for a mix of sitting and standing guests, with tables and chairs on the main floor. The living trees knit together in high-rafters and beams, which give the room a warmth as the sounds of musicians tuning up in preparation to win their Pipes bounce around its branches. A quiet din of chatter gives an energy to the room, the audience excited for the quality of show they are about to witness.

For those that pass through the city, the Ionian is a must visit location with unforgettable experiences for many. But those that return on more than one occasion may be surprised to find the inside shifted and warped into a new layout. The inside appears to be much larger than the outside would imply possible, lending to the arcane and magic essence of the place.

The proprietors are an unexpected pairing; behind the bar stands a 6'6'' Orc, his skin an ashen green, dressed in a maroon three piece suit with a forest green cravat and wide brimmed hat that folds up on one side, a long feather of a mysterious creature attached under a band the crowning touch. Beneath his hat, his head is clean cut along the sides with a long fauxhawk down the middle, tied into a braid at the back. Mingling through the crowd, chatting affably with customers wearing gleaming pins of silver Pipes, is a halfling man in a green filigreed smoking jacket and brown waistcoat. A well kept moustache and goatee are starting to speckle with white, his hair a well kept mop on top with sides tapered short.

**How It Works**:  
The modestly sized stage is open to anyone to perform on most nights. Those that wish to win their Pipes are judged by the reactions of those who have already won them. If a bard votes for the performer, they release their resonance, a magical effect from their Pipes that releases a small light that pulses with the song or story. If the performance receives enough, the artist earns their Pipes. If not, they are welcome to come back and try again. Those with their Pipes hold petitioners to a high standard in order to join their ranks. They are not politically motivated, but creatively, only accepting those of the highest skill.

**Why It Matters**:  
Pipe bearers are recognised performers and storytellers, often welcomed in courts, bardic colleges, and upper society circles. Their performances may be remembered for generations, and can often get them into places that few are able to tread. Providing background music or event entertainment for the rich and nobility, they have the unique opportunity to see and hear more than most.

**In-Game Effects**:
- Gain **advantage on Performance** in noble, academic, or artistic settings

**Game Hooks**:
- A legendary set of Pipes has been stolen, and the thief is mimicking its owner’s performances for manipulation
- A noble offers a reward for a new ballad that can move a grieving child to smile again
- A rival bard is sabotaging newcomers to preserve their monopoly on fame
