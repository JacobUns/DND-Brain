# Canticle of Shards
Before the stars,  
Before the flame,  
Two great spirits moved in silence.

Morwyn dreamed of children.  
She whispered her longing,  
And Veylan answered.

But the womb was barren.  
Her hope withered,  
Her grief sharpened,  
And in her sorrow she tore herself open.  
She cast her womb into the void.

Too late she knew her loss.  
She fell, broken, upon her own gift.  
And where she lay—  
the world was born.

Her skin became the soil.  
Her hair, the forests.  
Her bones, the mountains.  
Her blood, the oceans.  
Her eyes shone as the twin moons.  
And her soul shattered into countless spirits,  
scattered across all that lives.

Veylan wept.  
He sat in the dark, hollow with grief.  
But from Morwyn’s body  
life began to stir.  
Small voices, trembling,  
children without a mother’s hand.

He knew them as their own.  
So he tore himself apart—  
pieces of his soul  
becoming spirits  
to guide, protect, and teach.

With the last of his strength,  
he split the void  
and kindled the stars.

Then Veylan lay down,  
his watch eternal.  
His body blazed into the sun

# The Birth of the World
In the beginning, when there was only the void, there dwelled two great spirits: *Morwyn* and *Veylan*.

Morwyn, longing for life and companionship, spoke to Veylan of her desire to bear children. Veylan agreed with joy, yet despite their love, Morwyn could not conceive. Grief consumed her heart, and in her sorrow and fury she tore her own womb from within and cast it into the endless void. Too late, she realised what she had done. Her strength failed, and she fell upon her own womb.

Where she lay broken, her body gave birth to the world:
- Her skin became the soil
- Her hair, the forests.
- Her bones, the mountains.
- Her blood, the oceans and rivers.
- Her eyes became the twin moons.
- And her soul shattered into countless fragments, becoming the many spirits that dwell in all things.

When Veylan beheld this, he fell into despair. For a long age he mourned. Yet in time, he saw life stirring upon her womb; tiny, fragile beings living in darkness and fear. He knew then that these were the children he and Morwyn had longed for.

Moved by love, he resolved to ease their suffering. Tearing fragments from his own soul, he shaped new spirits to guide and guard their children. With the last of his strength, he pierced the void and filled it with stars, so that their children might never again be lost in darkness.

At last, spent and sorrowful yet at peace, *Veylan* gave himself wholly. His body became the radiant sun, watching always over the world born of his beloved *Morwyn*.
### Large known shards

- *Elythra* — The Binding Spirit — keeper of balance, oaths, and law.
- *Saedra* — The Spring-Flame — spirit of dawn’s light, the warmth that melts winter, bringer of renewal and planting fires.
- *Nythor* — The Great Stag — patron of hunters, survival, and martial discipline. Teacher of the hunt, of ki, and the first arts of combat. Favored spirit of Oda.
- *Velis* — The Spider-Mother — spinner of webs and fates, weaving beginnings to endings.
- *Thyros* — _Forgelight — spirit of craft, smithing, and invention.
- *Calenne* — The Fox-Spirit — patron of roads, trade, and passage, the cunning guide who knows hidden paths.
- *Beyra* — The Bear-Mother — spirit of hearth, kinship, and shelter, whose embrace is a den in winter.
- *Tuulen Henki* — The Storm-Shape — a restless spirit of thunder, rain, and shifting winds, who takes many forms to walk among mortals.
- *Ithar* — The Withering Hand — plague, rot, and unmaking.
- *Hythen* — The Reveler — excess, greed, and desire unchecked.
- *Maleth* — The Flame-Tongue — lies, chaos, and cruel delight.
- *Morgren* — The Hidden Knife — betrayal, secrets, and silent death.
- *Branna* — _The Crow — spirit of silence, winter, and the gathering of souls.      

### Small Spirits 
- *Hearth-Flame Daughter* – Keeper of warmth and food, fragment of Beyra. Fed with crumbs and drink.
    - *Web-Sisters* – Small spider-spirits, kin of Velis. Their webs are never destroyed without apology.
    - *Mouse-Shadow* – Protector of pantries and barns, shard of Nythor. Honored with grain scattered on the floor.
    - *Stone-Ear of the Road* – Dwells in boulders by paths; fragment of Calenne. Cairns are raised in its honor.
    - *Root-Keeper* – Spirit of tree roots, shard of Elythra. Farmers pour milk into the soil at planting.
    - *Field-Whisper* – A wind that bends the crops just before harvest; origin unknown. Some say it’s Morwyn’s sigh.
    - *Spring-Child* – Playful water-spirit, shard of Saedra. Offerings of flowers thrown into wells and springs.
    - *Branch-Singers* – Tiny wind voices in trees, shards of Tuulen Henki. Appeased with feathers tied to branches.
    - *Mist-Lanterns* – Small lights seen on foggy nights; origin unknown. Believed to be lost souls needing guidance.
    - *Crow’s Watcher* – A lone crow-feather shrine-spirit, shard of Branna. Paid with shiny beads and trinkets.
    - *Hearth-Dog* – Protector of families and children, often honored when a dog lies before the fire. Unknown origin.
- *Whisper-Stone* – Rocks that “hum” or “sing” when struck; spirit origin unknown. Villagers leave coins or rings.
    - *The White Cricket* – Rare, pale crickets sometimes seen in summer. Their chirp is said to foretell good fortune. Origin wholly unknown.      
## Gems as Shards
The Odan treatment of gemstones is perhaps their most curious custom. To them, such stones are more than trinkets; they are fragments of Morwyn and Veylan’s very souls.
- Rubies are Morwyn’s blood.
- Sapphires are Veylan’s tears.
- Diamonds are fragments of their hearts.

The Odans thus adorn themselves not with symbols of wealth but with supposed remnants of their “gods.” Though crude in concept, this reveals a strikingly spiritual instinct — to hold beauty not as ornament but as sacred presence.

The Odan people thus choose not to adorn themselves with symbols of wealth, though there are some who still do, but to instead adorn themselves with symbols of their faith and belief. in the spirits of their land.

### The Shamans of Oda
Shamans in Oda are regarded as the mediators between people and spirits, walking a line that is both sacred and perilous. Each shaman forges bonds with spirits of all kinds — from the great shards of Morwyn and Veylan to the smallest presences in stones, streams, or winds. These bonds allow shamans to call upon the powers of the spirits, yet each use leaves its mark. The deeper the connection, the more a shaman begins to take on traits of their patrons — bark like skin from a tree-spirit, eyes like stormlight from a wind-spirit, or voices that echo like flowing water. To channel too much at once risks dissolving entirely, their bodies scattering into golden dust.

No two shamans are alike. Some serve at shrines dedicated to particular spirits, much like caretakers, living close to the sacred places where people come with offerings and prayers. Others dwell in groves or wild places, attuned to the rhythms of the land itself. A few live as wanderers, carrying their spirits with them wherever they go, serving villages in need of healing, guidance, or protection. Tools vary from shaman to shaman — drums to summon rhythm and heartbeat, wind chimes to call unseen presences, or carved idols to anchor a spirit in the world. Each shaman’s practice is as unique as the spirits they bind themselves to.

The shamans honor [[Suuri Sankari]] as their founder, for it is said he gave them the first teachings and bound them under his protection. His descendants still receive reverence from the shamanic orders, who view them as the living heirs of his pact. To this day, the shamans see themselves not as rulers, but as keepers of balance, ensuring that spirits and mortals live in accord rather than conflict.

Over the centuries, the [[Pavella]] have become the hands of the shamans. Once famed as monster-hunters during Suuri’s time, their role gradually shifted. Today they act as agents, protectors, and executioners, carrying out tasks the shamans themselves cannot — from silencing threats to enforcing pacts, to defending sacred sites. To outsiders they are whispered of as assassins, but within Oda they are viewed as necessary shadows, the sharpened edge of shamanic authority. Their loyalty is not to crowns or noble houses, but to the spirits and the shamans who speak for them.

## *The Rite of Shaping Blood*

Among the most secret and sacred duties of Oda’s shamans is the *selection of marriage partners for the descendants of Suuri Sankari*. Unlike common unions, which are often guided by families, spirits, or even personal choice, the bloodline of Suuri is treated as something closer to a living relic — a vessel that must be guarded and shaped for the good of the city. The shamans, acting as both matchmakers and spirit-guides, consult omens, dreams, and ancestral whispers to determine suitable partners. These choices are rarely based on wealth or politics alone; instead, they hinge upon how well the union might preserve, or even strengthen, the memory of Suuri himself.

When a descendant’s consort becomes pregnant, the shamans enact a closely guarded rite known as the *Rite of Shaping Blood. Over several nights, the shamans gather in Sanctuary Hill’s hidden shrine, calling upon spirits of memory, ancestry, and flesh. Through chants, offerings, and the burning of rare herbs, they bind the unborn child to the **image of Suuri Sankari*, shaping the body so that the newborn may resemble the city’s founder as closely as possible. This practice is seen not as vanity, but as necessity — a way of ensuring that Suuri remains ever-present among his people, even through generations.

Yet the ritual is never perfect. No matter how powerful the magic or how precise the shamans’ guidance, the children always emerge with *marks of difference*: an unusual hue to the eyes, hair with a faint tint of copper or silver, a stature slightly taller or shorter than expected, a voice with a strange timbre, or some quirk of the spirit that sets them apart. These subtle deviations are spoken of in whispers — some see them as flaws, others as signs of individuality, proof that even Suuri’s blood cannot be fully bound by ritual. Among the common folk, it is said these “differences” are fragments of Morwyn and Veylan’s shattered souls bleeding through, reminding Oda that even in shaping perfection, the spirits leave their mark.

### *Nythor, the Stag-Spirit*

*Nythor* is remembered in Oda as both a teacher and a reminder of the world’s cruelty. Unlike gentler spirits who whisper of balance and beauty, Nythor embodies the *harsh truth of survival*: only the strong endure, and life is paid for with life. When the first people trembled in the wilds, weak and starving, it was Nythor who came to them. He did not give comfort; instead, he showed them how to stalk prey, how to read the winds and earth, how to strike without hesitation, and how to eat what they killed. From him they learned that the world does not care for the frail — but that by strength, cunning, and skill, they could endure.

His lessons were merciless but essential. He taught that every kill must be met with respect, not pity, for in taking the life of another one acknowledges their own place in the cycle of blood. He showed them which roots could nourish, which plants could poison, and how to see in the eyes of animals their own reflection as hunter and hunted alike. To Nythor, a wolf has fangs and a stag has antlers — but humans were not left defenseless. Their weapon was *ki, the current that runs through all things. By shaping it with discipline, they could strike with the force of storm and endure with the strength of stone. From this teaching grew both the art of hunting and the first forms of **martial practice*: the human way to bare their own fangs against the world.

In the City of Oda, Nythor’s main shrine stands in the Temple District. Its centerpiece is a statue unlike the serene forms of other spirits: a *wild, man-shaped figure draped in stag’s skin*, its antlers jagged, its hands and forearms carved in perpetual blood-red to the elbows. Worshippers leave offerings of meat, antler carvings, or hunting tools, acknowledging his truth — that life is won through struggle. Many pavella and martial students pray there before hunts, duels, or journeys, seeking his favor not for mercy but for strength to survive.

Because of this, Nythor remains one of Oda’s most respected yet feared spirits. He is not loved, but he is *honored*: the people know that without his harshness, they would never have learned to live in the world’s teeth.

## The Pavella

The Pavella are more than martial elites; they are the shadows and guardians of Oda’s spiritual and political order. Rooted in traditions said to stretch back to [[Suuri Sankari]] himself, they began as monster-hunters and protectors of shamans. Over centuries, their role shifted into something far broader: assassins, guardians, and agents who serve the shamans and Suuri’s descendants with absolute loyalty. To be chosen for the Pavella is considered a great honor. All children of Oda are trained from youth in martial discipline, but only those of exceptional promise are selected to enter their ranks.

### Rituals and Symbolism

Upon induction, initiates undertake the *Oath of Strands*, cutting a lock of their hair and burning it with powdered gems in a shrine dedicated to [[Suuri Sankari]]. This act binds their life to service, and their remaining hair becomes sacred — never cut except in ritual. Breaking this vow is an act of dishonor punishable by exile. Pavella also honor the spirits through personal totems: animal tattoos hidden beneath their robes, sashes patterned with rainfall for Suuri’s blade [[Rain Cutter]], or charms carved from bone or wood. Each mark is a reminder that their lives are tied not only to their masters, but to the spirit-world itself.

### Clothing

A Pavella’s clothing is personal, reflecting their own spirit, temperament, and chosen symbols. Some craft flowing silks dyed in storm-colors, elegant garments that move with grace and conceal subtle armor. Others choose furs, feathers, or coarse weaves, drawing themselves closer to the wild animals they revere. A Sanctuary Guard might wear fine robes embroidered with the glitter-gold butterfly and lotus of Suuri’s line, while another Pavella might walk in wolf-pelts, carrying the presence of the hunt. What unites them is not uniformity, but the spiritual weight their clothing carries — each garment is both armor and declaration.

Exile still strips a Pavella of honor, but not of choice. A banished warrior may keep their silks or furs, yet without the bond of oath, those garments are emptied of their meaning. Many such outcasts become mercenaries or wanderers, feared as much for what they once were as for what they have become
## Famous Branches of the Pavella

Though united under one name, the Pavella divide into several orders, each with its own character and responsibilities.

•⁠  ⁠*The Sanctuary Guard* – The most famous of the Pavella, tasked with guarding the descendants of [[Suuri Sankari]]. Their robes are the most ornate, often trimmed with embroidered lotuses, glitter-gold butterfly and storm-patterns. They are celebrated in song and tale, though their duty is deadly serious.
    
•⁠  ⁠*The Mice* – Servants in appearance, assassins in truth. They blend into the households of Suuri’s line as cooks, attendants, and scribes, but spring into action if their masters are threatened. Their attire is subdued, made to appear humble and practical, yet always concealing blades and poisons. The name “Mice” was given not as an insult, but in awe — for like mice, they are everywhere, unseen, and impossible to root out.
    
•⁠  ⁠*The Wall Guardians* – Each of Oda’s four great walls is watched by a company of Pavella, each led by a captain. Their robes are reinforced with heavier stitching and leather linings, giving them a slightly more martial look. They are respected for their collaboration with Oda’s soldiers and feared for their mercilessness in rooting out threats.
    
•⁠  ⁠*The Silent Ones* – They have no official name, only the one whispered among the people. This branch acts as Oda’s secret police, watching the streets for disloyalty or treachery. Their attire is nearly featureless: dark, plain, and without ornamentation. They are rarely spoken of openly, for to do so invites suspicion, and it is said that those taken by the Silent Ones are never seen again.
    
•⁠  ⁠*The Eastern Watch* – Stationed at the edge of the Forbidden Zone, they are tasked with ensuring no monster — or man — crosses into Oda. Their robes are lighter and travel-stained, practical for long patrols in harsh weather. They are considered among the most hardened of the Pavella, for their eyes are always turned toward the storm.

### The Sacredness of Hair

Among the people of Oda, hair is regarded as the *living thread of the self* — it grows with a person through every season of life, carrying the memory of their joys, struggles, and oaths. To cut one’s hair is never a casual act; it is a statement, a severing of what once was, or a vow of what will be. Warriors may offer a lock of hair before battle to bind themselves to their comrades, while shamans sometimes cut their hair in sacrifice when seeking the attention of a great spirit. To give away one’s hair is to give away a part of one’s story, and thus it is considered the most truthful offering a person can make.

Hair is treated with reverence in daily life. It is washed carefully in river water believed to hold echoes of Morwyn’s blood and combed with oils infused with herbs tied to different spirits. Many choose to let their hair grow long, for each added length is a sign of endurance, patience, and unbroken continuity. Cutting it too short without cause may be seen as careless or disrespectful, unless done for mourning, oath-making, or renewal of the self.

Adornment is also deeply important. Beads, combs, and pins are set into braids, often inlaid with gems and stones believed to be shards of Morwyn and Veylan’s sundered bodies. A sapphire might be worn to seek guidance, a green stone for growth, quartz for balance, and diamonds for clarity of spirit. To weave such stones into the hair is not fashion alone, but a way of aligning the self with the world’s hidden order, carrying fragments of the first spirits with every step.
